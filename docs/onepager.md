================================================================================
THE LENS - FIELD CARD
A simple way to look at anything: a person, a company, a choice, a relationship.
Goal: understand it, guess where it's going, and decide if (and how) to push it.
================================================================================

THE BIG IDEA (in one breath)
  Don't see a "thing." See a thing that MOVES.
  Ask, in order: What is it? -> Where is it heading? -> Can I move it? -> Should I?
  And never forget: your view is a MAP, not the territory. Maps can be wrong.

THE ONE TRAP TO AVOID
  Seeing two things move together is NOT the same as knowing one causes the other.
  "They go together" (what you watched) =/= "push this and that happens" (what you
  want). Before calling anything a "lever," ask: how do I KNOW pushing it works?
  If your only proof is "they move together," you don't know yet.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE 8 QUESTIONS (run these on anything, top to bottom)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. WHOSE view, paid by whom - and should I even analyze this? (Some things you
     should just live, not dissect.)
  2. DOES IT REACT TO BEING WATCHED? People, markets, and companies change when
     they know you're modeling them. Rocks and weather don't. This changes everything.
  3. WHAT KIND OF PROBLEM IS THIS?
       - Obvious -> just use the known best way.
       - Complicated -> bring in an expert to analyze it.
       - Complex (most people/business stuff) -> you CAN'T figure it out in advance.
         Run small safe experiments and learn from what works.
       - Chaos/crisis -> act first to stop the bleeding, think later.
  4. WHAT REALLY DESCRIBES IT? Pick the few things that actually matter. Most
     stuck problems are just "looking at the wrong things."
  5. WHERE IS IT ON THE MAP? Is it stable, or near a cliff (a sudden flip:
     crash, breakup, collapse)? Warning sign of a coming flip: it gets jumpy and
     slow to recover from small knocks.
  6. WHAT DO I ACTUALLY KNOW vs. JUST GUESS? And the big one: can this WIPE ME
     OUT? Avoid ruin first - you only get one life/one shot; a blow-up cancels
     all future wins, no matter how good the odds looked.
  7. CAN I KNOW IT? CAN I MOVE IT? (See the box below.)
  8. WHAT DID I MISS, and is my fancy view actually better than a dumb guess? If
     not, drop the fancy view.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE MAIN TOOL: the 2x2 (the most useful thing here)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Two separate questions: Can I KNOW it? Can I MOVE it? They're not the same.

                    | I CAN MOVE IT          | I CAN'T MOVE IT
  ------------------+------------------------+----------------------------
  I CAN SEE IT      | ACT on it. Find the    | GET OUT OF THE WAY. You see
                    | real lever. Be patient | it coming but can't stop it.
                    | (effects can lag).     | Protect yourself / step aside.
  ------------------+------------------------+----------------------------
  I CAN'T SEE IT    | MEASURE FIRST. Don't   | LET IT GO. Stop fighting it.
                    | act blind - build a    | Stay flexible, keep options,
                    | way to see it.         | and walk away.
  Most mistakes: people act as if they're in the wrong box and don't realize it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SIMPLE RULES THAT SAVE YOU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Survive first. Never risk what you can't afford to lose, even for great odds.
  * When you can't predict, prepare. Keep options open, make small reversible
    bets, cut the downside, leave the upside open.
  * What gets measured gets gamed. The moment a number becomes the goal, people
    twist it. (So don't trust the number blindly.)
  * Push gently on slow systems. Pushing harder/faster on something with a delay
    often makes it swing out of control. Wait for the lag.
  * Some things don't reverse. Lost trust, a ruined name, an extinction - you
    can't just undo the cause and get the thing back. Be extra careful near these.
  * Trust changes the result. The same advice lands differently from someone
    trusted vs. someone not. Trust is real fuel - don't burn it.
  * Tag your facts: KNOWN / GUESSED / MADE-UP. Don't let a guess wear the
    costume of a fact.
  * Beat the dumb baseline. If your big model can't beat "same as last time" or
    "what usually happens," it's decoration.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
KNOW WHEN TO PUT THE LENS DOWN (important)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  This is a tool, not a religion. Pick it up, then set it down.
  Ask: "Does looking at this coldly RUIN it?"
  Don't run the math on love, friendship, grief, play, or your own meaning.
  A life optimized like a spreadsheet isn't a better life.
  Use the lens on what it helps. Just live the rest.

================================================================================
Bottom line: See things as moving, not fixed. Don't confuse "goes together" with
"causes." Survive first. Know what you can change and what you can't. And know
when to stop analyzing and just live.
================================================================================
