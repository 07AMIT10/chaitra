A pairing is only viable if it resists rebellion. Can decentralized proposals and tentative holds guarantee an outcome where no clandestine alliance threatens the peace?

## The Anatomy of an Elopement

Consider any proposed matching between two camps. What condition makes it unstable?

A pair $(L, R)$ is a **blocking pair** if $L$ prefers $R$ over their assigned partner *and* $R$ prefers $L$ over their assigned partner. If even one such rogue pair exists, both individuals have active incentive to defect, shatter the social contract, and elope.

A matching is **stable** if and only if it contains zero blocking pairs. Exhaustively auditing an existing matching is simple ($O(n^2)$ checks), but constructing an assignment guaranteed to possess no rogue pairs is a non-trivial feat of game theory.

## The Conway–Knuth Lattice

The mathematical input is not a standard graph, but two families of strict linear orders. The set of all valid stable matchings for a given market forms a geometric structure: a distributive **lattice** (Conway; Knuth 1976).

Define a partial order on stable matchings by proposer welfare: matching $M_1 \succeq M_2$ if every proposer ranks their partner in $M_1$ at least as high as in $M_2$. Under this order, any two stable matchings possess a stable meet (the point-wise receiver compromise) and a stable join (the point-wise proposer compromise).

The two extreme poles of this lattice are generated by swapping who proposes. When left proposes, you land on the lattice summit; when right proposes, you land on the lattice floor.

## The Proposer Advantage and Asymmetric Equilibrium

**Theorem (Gale & Shapley 1962).** The deferred acceptance algorithm always terminates, and the terminal assignment contains zero blocking pairs.

Termination is guaranteed by simple monotonic counting. Proposers never propose to the same receiver twice, descending strictly down their preference lists. The process must halt in at most $|L| \times |R|$ total proposals (McVitie & Wilson 1971).

Why is the terminal state immune to mutiny? Suppose a proposer $a$ and receiver $w$ secretly prefer each other over their assigned matches. Because $a$ ranks $w$ above their final match, $a$ must have already proposed to $w$ earlier in the run, only to be rejected in favour of someone $w$ desired even more.

**Proposer Optimality (Gale & Shapley 1962; Dubins & Freedman 1981).** Every proposer receives the best partner they could possibly secure in *any* stable matching, while every receiver receives their worst possible partner among all stable matchings.

## Medical Residencies, Kidney Exchanges, and Market Design

In 1952, American medical schools and residency hospitals faced rampant unraveling: hospitals made binding offers to medical students years in advance to preempt competitors. The National Resident Matching Program (NRMP) adopted deferred acceptance, revolutionising clinical assignments nationwide (Roth 1984).

The same mechanism powers urban public school assignment systems in Boston and New York, matching hundreds of thousands of students to schools without black-market swaps.

Paired kidney exchange registries adapt these equilibrium mechanics to save lives, clearing cyclical barter chains across incompatible donor-patient pairs.

## Strategic Manipulation and the Rural Hospitals Invariant

Deferred acceptance is **strategy-proof** for the proposing side: no proposer can improve their partner by misrepresenting their true preference rankings (Dubins & Freedman 1981; Roth 1982). Proposers have zero incentive to lie.

Receivers, however, can game the system: a receiver can strategically truncate or permute their rankings to force proposers further down their lists, climbing higher up the lattice.

**The Rural Hospitals Theorem (Roth 1986).** The exact set of agents matched is invariant across all stable matchings in the lattice! Rural hospitals that go unfilled in the doctor-optimal matching will also go unfilled in the hospital-optimal matching; changing who proposes never creates new matches for underserved institutions.
